================================================================================
SUPPLY CHAIN PERFORMANCE ANALYTICS — BUSINESS INSIGHTS
================================================================================

This report summarizes the key findings from the analysis of 100 SKUs across
skincare, haircare, and cosmetics product categories. Each section states an
observation, the supporting numbers, and a practical recommendation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. REVENUE DISTRIBUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Observation:
  Skincare is the top revenue-generating category, followed by haircare and
  cosmetics.

Numbers:
  - Total revenue (all SKUs): $577,604.82
  - Skincare: $241,628.16 (41.8% of total, 40 SKUs)
  - Haircare: $174,455.39 (30.2% of total, 34 SKUs)
  - Cosmetics: $161,521.27 (28.0% of total, 26 SKUs)
  - Top SKU: SKU51 (haircare) at $9,866.47

Implication:
  Skincare drives the largest share of revenue. However, cosmetics has a higher
  average revenue per SKU ($6,212) compared to haircare ($5,131), suggesting
  cosmetics products individually perform well despite having fewer SKUs.

Recommendation:
  Prioritize skincare inventory and marketing. Evaluate whether expanding the
  cosmetics SKU count could increase total revenue given its strong per-SKU
  performance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PRODUCT PROFITABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Observation:
  44 out of 100 SKUs (44%) are loss-making, meaning their manufacturing cost
  exceeds the selling price.

Numbers:
  - Formula: Unit Gross Profit = Selling Price - Manufacturing Cost
  - Profitable SKUs: 56 | Loss-making SKUs: 44
  - Average Unit Gross Profit by category:
      Cosmetics: +$14.31
      Skincare:  -$1.73
      Haircare:  -$2.44
  - Top performer: SKU14 (skincare), +$93.38 per unit
  - Worst performer: SKU23 (cosmetics), -$94.29 per unit
    (Price: $4.32, Manufacturing Cost: $98.61)

Implication:
  Nearly half of all products lose money on every unit sold. Haircare and
  skincare categories have negative average unit gross profits, while cosmetics
  is the only category with a positive average.

Recommendation:
  Review pricing and supplier contracts for the 44 loss-making SKUs. Products
  like SKU23 and SKU4 need immediate attention — either increase the selling
  price, renegotiate manufacturing costs, or discontinue the product.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SUPPLIER DEFECT RATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Observation:
  Supplier 5 has the highest average defect rate, while Supplier 1 has the
  lowest. The gap between them is meaningful.

Numbers:
  - Supplier 5: 2.67% avg defect rate (highest)
  - Supplier 3: 2.47%
  - Supplier 2: 2.36%
  - Supplier 4: 2.34%
  - Supplier 1: 1.80% avg defect rate (lowest)
  - By category: Haircare has the highest avg defect rate (2.48%), cosmetics
    the lowest (1.92%).

Implication:
  Supplier 5 produces 48% more defects per unit than Supplier 1 on average.
  This increases costs through returns, rework, and customer dissatisfaction.

Recommendation:
  Conduct a quality audit of Supplier 5. If defect rates cannot be reduced,
  consider shifting production volume to Supplier 1, which consistently
  delivers the lowest defect rates.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SHIPPING COST EFFICIENCY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Observation:
  Shipping costs are broadly similar across the three carriers, but Carrier B
  is consistently the cheapest.

Numbers:
  - Carrier B: avg $5.51 per unit (cheapest)
  - Carrier A: avg $5.55 per unit
  - Carrier C: avg $5.60 per unit (most expensive)

Implication:
  The differences between carriers are small ($0.09 between cheapest and most
  expensive). On 100 SKUs this is not transformative, but at scale the
  savings would accumulate.

Recommendation:
  For cost optimization at scale, negotiate volume discounts with Carrier B.
  The carrier cost differences alone do not justify a full carrier switch,
  but should be considered alongside delivery reliability data if available.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. MANUFACTURING LEAD TIME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Observation:
  Supplier 5 has the longest average manufacturing lead time, while Supplier 1
  is the fastest.

Numbers:
  - Supplier 5: 16.3 days avg lead time (slowest)
  - Supplier 2: 15.6 days
  - Supplier 4: 15.3 days
  - Supplier 3: 14.9 days
  - Supplier 1: 12.6 days avg lead time (fastest)
  - By category: Haircare has the longest lead time (17.1 days), cosmetics
    the shortest (13.3 days).

Implication:
  Supplier 1 manufactures products ~30% faster than Supplier 5. Combined with
  Supplier 1's lowest defect rate, it is the most operationally efficient
  supplier in the dataset.

Recommendation:
  Allocate more production volume to Supplier 1 where possible. For haircare
  products (which have the longest lead times), investigate whether
  manufacturing process improvements or alternative suppliers could reduce
  production time.
